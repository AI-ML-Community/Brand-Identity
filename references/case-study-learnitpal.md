# Case study: the LearnitPal mark

LearnitPal is a reading app. It picks the one article worth your time today, lets you swipe to pass or keep, and remembers your place. The files for every stage are in `../examples/learnitpal/`.

## 1. Raw materials came from the product

The app already had a visual system, so the identity honoured it:
- **colours:** warm ink `#1B1712`, amber `#E0962C` (dark-mode amber `#F0AE45`), paper `#F9F5EF`
- **fonts:** Plus Jakarta Sans for the UI, Newsreader as the serif
- **screens:** the Today screen features a single amber "sun"

From the product:
- **nouns:** article, card, pick, bookmark, place, queue, timing window
- **verbs:** read, keep, pass, save, come back
- **ritual:** one pick a day, kept for later
- **the name:** an L to start with; "pal" gives a warm, companion tone

## 2. Clichés banned up front

Open book, lightbulb, graduation cap, brain, pencil, speech bubble, sparkles, gradient, globe. Naming them made it impossible to drift back into them.

## 3. Three concepts, rendered side by side

See `renders/1-explore.png`.

| Concept | Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading nook** | A bookmark ribbon (V-notch on top, swallowtail at the foot) folded into an L, cradling an amber dot | **Kept.** Object + letter + promise in one shape. |
| Page fold | A page with an amber folded corner, "the one worth keeping" | Rejected. Any notes, docs or PDF app could use it, so it fails the swap test. |
| Highlight | Three lines, the middle one amber | Rejected. It reads as a hamburger menu or a text-align icon. Generic. |

## 4. Refinement, one change per variant

See `renders/2-refine.png` and `renders/2-refine-tiny.png`.

| Variant | Change | Result |
|---|---|---|
| a | V-notch top, **square foot** | **Winner.** Simplest, and still carries the idea. |
| b | Rounded foot | Softer, but the rounded end makes it read like a "b" or a pipe, and it muddies the hard bookmark geometry. |
| d | 45° folded crease with a gap | Pretty large, but the gap disappears at 128px. |
| e | Two-tone fold (darker underside) | Needs two inks, fails in one colour, and the tone disappears when small. |

The swallowtail foot from exploration was dropped too: two notches competed with each other, and one notch is enough to say "bookmark".

## 5. The system

Everything is a ratio of the stroke width **T = 92** on a 272×304 grid:
- the mark is **3T wide × 3.3T tall**
- the notch is **0.43T** deep (40 units), cut at the stroke's midpoint
- the dot's radius is **⅔T** (62), centred so it clears both arms by **¼T** (22). It rests in the corner without touching.
- clear space is **½T** on every side

Constraints held: three shapes (two, in fact), two colours, no curves in the ribbon, no gradients. It prints in one colour, and in that version the dot takes the ink colour too.

## 6. Wordmark

- "learnitpal" is outlined from `plus_jakarta_sans_bold.ttf`, with HarfBuzz kerning and **−14 units of tracking** per glyph (per 1000 em).
- It is lowercase, for a friendly, companion tone that suits "pal".
- **The one echo:** the i's tittle is replaced with an amber circle at 1.08× the original dot's size, because circles read smaller than squares. The name and the symbol now share the same "one pick" dot.

## 7. Lockup balance: the fix after looking

- **First render:** the mark was 1.3× the l's height, with a gap of 1.05T. It looked too heavy, because the mark's stroke was 2.2× the font's stem.
- **Fix:** the mark went down to **1.16× the ascender** and the gap up to **1.25T**.
- **App icon:** at scale 1.55 the mark looked lost on the tile. It went up to **1.72**, nudged +10px right and −6px up for optical centre.

## 8. Deliverables and verification

- **Files:** 17 SVGs plus PNG previews, all generated by `build.py`, and a brand sheet generated by `sheet.py`.
- **First sheet screenshot:** the "0.43T" label was clipped by the construction diagram's viewBox. The viewBox was widened and the check re-run.
- **Tag check:** passed.
- **Report to the user:** the idea, why it isn't generic, the file paths, and what wasn't done (wiring the icon and splash into Android).

## What made it work, in one line each

- It was designed from the product's ritual (one pick, keep your place), not its category (learning).
- One shape does three jobs: object, letter, promise.
- Every decision was made by looking at renders, including throwing away two concepts.
- Each variant changed one thing, and everything was tested at small sizes and on dark.
- All proportions come from one unit, and every file comes from one script.
- One echo detail connects the wordmark to the mark.
